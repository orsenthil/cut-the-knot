Disappearing Rectangles

Puzzle by Alexander Bogomolny (cut-the-knot.org)

Game: a strip of paper with rectangles drawn on it is cut in two along the middle. Slide the halves and make a rectangle disappear.
How to play: drag the top or bottom half sideways (or use the arrow buttons), then count the rectangles.
Top: Bottom:
Look for the math

Sam Loyd's "Get Off the Earth" made a whole warrior vanish this way. This strip is the same trick stripped down, and it is a lesson in conservation: nothing really disappears.

How the strip is made

With N rectangles, the top parts are 0, 1, 2, …, N−1 units tall in some order, and each bottom part makes its rectangle up to N−1 units. So one top part is empty (its rectangle is all below the cut) and one bottom part is empty (all above). Tick "Show units" to count them.

Where the rectangle goes

Slide the top half until its empty piece sits over the empty bottom piece. That column is now blank, and you count N−1 rectangles. The amount of blue never changed: it is N × (N−1) units at every position of the strips. Shared among N−1 rectangles instead of N, each one is on average a whole unit taller. The missing rectangle was cut into pieces and spread over the others, and our eyes count shapes, not area. Checking every possible sliding position, on thousands of random strips, finds exactly one position with a rectangle missing.

N(N−1) = (N−1) × N: N rectangles of height N−1, or N−1 of average height N.

Try it with 3 rectangles, then 20. With many rectangles the extra unit is spread so thin that the trick is very hard to see.
