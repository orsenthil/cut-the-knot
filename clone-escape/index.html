Escape of the Clones

Puzzle by Alexander Bogomolny (cut-the-knot.org)

Game: get every clone out of the orange prison in the corner. Click a clone and it splits in two: one copy moves down, one moves right.
How to play: a clone can only split if both squares — below it and to its right — are free. Start with the small prison and work up. Some of them cannot be escaped at all.
Look for the math

Three of the five prisons can be emptied and two cannot, and no amount of clicking will tell you which is which — the board is infinite, so "I couldn't do it" proves nothing. What settles it is a single number that never changes however you play. Turn on Show weights to see it being carried around.

Give every square a weight

Write 2−(row+col) on the square in that row and column: 1 in the corner, 1/2 on its two neighbours, 1/4 on the next diagonal, and so on forever. Now watch what a split does. A clone of weight 2−s becomes two clones one step further out, each worth 2−(s+1), and

2−(s+1) + 2−(s+1) = 2−s

so the total weight the clones are standing on is exactly the same after the move as before. That total is an invariant: one clone in the corner is worth 1, and every position you can ever reach from it is worth 1 too.

The whole board is only worth 4

Add up every square. The top row is 1 + 1/2 + 1/4 + …, a geometric series worth 2. The next row is half of that, the one after half again, and the rows together come to 2 + 1 + 1/2 + … = 4. So the infinite board holds a grand total of 4, and the prisons eat a lot of it:

1 square: 1  ·  3 squares: 2  ·  2×2: 9/4  ·  6 squares: 11/4  ·  10 squares: 13/4

Escaping means every clone ends up outside the prison, so the weight they carry — always 1 — has to fit in what is left of the 4. The 10-square prison leaves only 4 − 13/4 = 3/4, and 3/4 is not enough room for 1. That prison can never be emptied, and you now know it without trying a single move.

The awkward one

The 6-square prison leaves 4 − 11/4 = 5/4, which is more than 1, so the simple count does not decide it. Two extra observations finish the job. After the first split there is always exactly one clone in the top row and exactly one in the left column, and the best they can do is sit on the 1/8 squares just outside the fence — so a good chunk of that 5/4 is unreachable. And a finite pile of clones can never collect the whole weight of an infinite region anyway: any total that needs infinitely many squares is out of reach in finitely many moves. Squeeze from both sides and the room left really is under 1. So the 6-square prison is inescapable too, just less obviously.

Try this: set the prison to 1 square but the start to 2 rows. Three clones carry a weight of 2, the board outside that one square holds 3, and it works. Now keep 2 rows and ask for the 3-square prison — weight 2 against exactly 2 of room, and suddenly it is hopeless.
