Stones in Cups

Puzzle by Alexander Bogomolny (cut-the-knot.org)

Game: cups stand in a circle with stones in them. Keep scooping and sharing out stones until every single stone has piled up in one cup.
How to play: the arrow shows the cup your hand is at. Press One Move to pick up all its stones and drop them one at a time into the cups going round to the left; your hand follows the last stone it drops, and that is where the next move starts.
Moves: 0
Look for the math

Barry Cipra set this as problem #1388 in Mathematics Magazine in 1992: with n cups in a circle and k stones in every one of them, prove that repeating the scoop-and-share move must eventually pile all kn stones into the cup you began at. Try the circle above with no empty cups and it always happens — but the published proof had a hole in it, and this applet is where Bogomolny went looking for it.

You can always run the move backwards

Here is the move in reverse. Pick up the stones under your hand, then walk clockwise taking one stone out of each cup you pass, until you reach a cup that is empty; pour everything you are holding into that one. Check it against any move you just made and you will get back exactly where you were. The undo is forced — there is never a choice about it — and that is the key fact.

Finitely many positions, so you must go round in a circle

A position means the stone counts plus where your hand is, and with a fixed number of stones there are only finitely many positions. So a long enough run has to repeat one. Now use the undo: if two different positions led to the same one, undoing it would have to give both answers, and it cannot. So the run cannot be a lasso that loops back into its own middle — the repeat has to be the opening position itself. Every run is a closed cycle that comes home. That is why pressing Run above can always reach a verdict rather than spinning for ever: either the stones gather, or the opening position comes back round.

Where the proof breaks

The published solution finished like this: undo one move from the opening position and you get "all the stones in the original cup", so that position is on the cycle and must be reached. Try it. Undo from n full cups: you take the k stones under your hand, then one stone from each of the other n-1 cups, which is k + n - 1 stones — not kn of them, unless k = 1. The step only works for one stone per cup. Nathan Bowler pointed this out to Bogomolny, and the page now carries the correction.

A setup that never finishes

The applet was built to explore a wider version of the puzzle, where a run of cups starts out empty, and there it found something better than a gap in a proof — a genuine counterexample. Set the circle to 6 cups, 2 stones in each, 1 empty cup, hand starting at cup 1, and press Run:

After twelve moves the picture is the one you started with, rotated two cups along. Twelve more and it rotates two further. After 36 moves it is back exactly where it began — and the stones were never once all in the same cup.

So with empty cups allowed, the claim is simply false, and the cycle argument above is what proves this particular run will never gather: a position that has come back once will keep coming back for ever.

With no empty cups, watch how long the gathering takes as you turn the stones up: 6 cups with 1 stone each takes 33 moves, with 2 stones each it takes 746. Can you find a pair of settings that takes thousands?
