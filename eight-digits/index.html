Eight Digits Puzzle

Puzzle by Alexander Bogomolny (cut-the-knot.org)

Game: put the digits 1 to 8 in the eight circles so that no two numbers that come one after the other sit in circles that touch.
How to play: click a circle to drop in the next digit, starting with 1. Click the circle you have just filled to take that digit back.
Next digit: 1
Look for the math

Trial and error will get you there in the end, but the puzzle is nearly solved before you place a digit. Two counts do almost all of the work, and the second one turns the whole thing into a walk.

The two middle circles are forced

Count the links. The two middle circles touch six others each — every circle but one. Now count forbidden partners: the digit 4 must avoid both 3 and 5, and so it goes for 2, 3, 5, 6 and 7. Only 1 and 8 are different: they have a single forbidden partner each, 2 and 7.

So suppose some digit d sits in a middle circle. Its forbidden partners must all squeeze into the one circle that the middle circle does not touch — and one circle holds one digit. A digit with two forbidden partners cannot do it, so the middle circles hold 1 and 8. That also fixes two more: 7 goes in the one circle that 8's circle misses, and 2 in the one that 1's circle misses.

Turn the picture inside out

Now stop looking at the links that are drawn and look at the ones that are not — press Show safe links to see them. Consecutive digits must sit in circles joined by a dashed line, so reading your answer in the order 1, 2, 3, …, 8 traces a route that visits every circle exactly once: a Hamiltonian path in the complementary graph. There are only 11 dashed links for 8 circles, so there is very little room. The middle circles have exactly one dashed link each, and a route that visits a circle without stopping needs two — so they have to be the two ends of the route, which is the first argument again, in one line.

Only one answer

Search every way of filling the circles — there are 8! = 40320 — and exactly four survive. They are the same answer four times over: mirror it left to right, flip it top to bottom, or do both, and you get the other three. Swapping every digit d for 9 − d also keeps consecutive digits consecutive, and for this figure that is just the same answer turned upside down. So the puzzle has, as Bogomolny puts it, one solution up to reflection and rotation.

Try this: start by putting 1 and 8 in the two middle circles, then place 2 and 7 in the only circles left for them. Four digits remain and the corners almost fill themselves.
