The Vanishing Digit

Puzzle by Alexander Bogomolny (cut-the-knot.org)

Goal: see if I can guess your secret digit.
How to play: think of a 3-digit number and write it backwards. Subtract the smaller number from the bigger one. Tell me the first two digits of the answer, and I'll name the last one.
Look for the math

There's no magic and no peeking — the answer's shape is forced before you pick a single digit. Write your number as digits a, b, c. By place value it's 100a + 10b + c, and reversed it's 100c + 10b + a.

The middle digit cancels

Subtract one from the other:

(100a + 10b + c) − (100c + 10b + a) = 99a − 99c = 99(a − c)

The 10b terms are identical, so they vanish — your middle digit never matters. Whatever number you chose, the answer is 99 times the gap k = |a − c|, and k can only be 0 to 9.

Only ten possible answers

000, 099, 198, 297, 396, 495, 594, 693, 792, 891

Look at the pattern: the middle digit is always 9, and the first and last digits always add up to 9. You can see why by writing 99k as 100k − k = 100(k−1) + 90 + (10−k), which has digits k−1, 9 and 10−k.

Why the digits add up to 18

Add them: (k−1) + 9 + (10−k) = 18 for every k from 1 to 9. Every answer is a multiple of 9, and by the divisibility rule for 9 the digit sum of a multiple of 9 is itself a multiple of 9 — here the digital root is 9 and the sum is 18. That's the same idea behind casting out nines. So the hidden digit is simply 18 minus the two digits you told me. The one exception is 000 (when the first and last digits of your number matched): then the hidden digit is 0.

Try it: pick 3 different starting numbers and check that the middle digit of the answer is always 9.
