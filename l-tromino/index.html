Tiling Rectangles with L-Trominoes

Puzzle by Alexander Bogomolny (cut-the-knot.org)

Game: cover the whole rectangle with L-shaped pieces made of three squares. Which rectangles can be covered?
How to play: click three squares one after another to make an L: the second one is the corner. Change the size and try again.
Look for the math

An L-tromino is a three-square polyomino shaped like the letter L. Solomon Golomb and Alexander Soifer asked exactly which rectangles they can tile.

A first condition

Each piece covers 3 squares, so the number of squares must be a multiple of 3: one side has to be divisible by 3. A 4 by 5 board, with 20 squares, is hopeless. But that isn't enough: 3 by 3 and 3 by 5 have 9 and 15 squares, and still can't be covered. The smallest rectangle that works is 2 by 3, two Ls fitted together.

The whole answer

Write the sides as a ≤ b, both at least 2. The rectangle can be tiled exactly when

a = 3 and b is even, or a ≠ 3 and ab is divisible by 3.

So 3 by b needs an even b: it is built from 2 by 3 blocks. Once both sides are at least 4, any area divisible by 3 works, by gluing together the 2 by 3 blocks with a few clever pieces such as 5 by 6 and 5 by 9. A computer search over every board from 2 by 2 to 10 by 10 finds a tiling exactly where the rule says there is one.

Try 3 by 5: it has 15 squares, a multiple of 3, but every attempt leaves a gap. Then try 5 by 9.
