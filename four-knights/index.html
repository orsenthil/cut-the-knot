Four Knights

Puzzle by Alexander Bogomolny (cut-the-knot.org)

Game: two red and two blue knights sit on the corners of a 3×3 board. Swap the reds and the blues using only real chess knight moves.
How to play: tap a knight, then tap one of its glowing legal squares to move it there. The ring on the right shows the exact same knights — just laid out in a line instead of a grid.

Look for the math

This is an old puzzle Bogomolny calls simply "an old puzzle": swap two pairs of knights using nothing but legal chess moves, on a board too small for a knight to have many options.

Only two moves from anywhere

A knight moves in an L-shape, and on this cramped 3×3 board that shape is very restrictive: the centre square can't be reached by a knight at all, and every one of the other 8 squares has exactly two legal knight moves, both landing on other outer squares.

Eight squares, one cycle

Draw those connections as a graph — a dot for each square, a line for each legal move — and something nice happens: every square has degree exactly 2, and following the lines visits all 8 squares and returns to where you started. That's a Hamiltonian cycle hiding inside the knight's tangled-looking moves. The ring on the right is that cycle, drawn out straight. Once you see the board that way, "which squares can this knight reach?" stops being a chess question and becomes "which two squares are next to it in the ring?"

A puzzle about a line, not a board

On a plain line of 8 slots with 4 tokens and 4 gaps, sliding tokens into adjacent empty slots is a much more familiar kind of puzzle than knight moves on a chessboard — and now they're the same puzzle. Every move you make on either board does exactly the same thing to the shared position underneath.

One question the original page leaves open, worth sitting with: what is the fewest moves this can be solved in?

A different target

The original page asks one more question, and doesn't answer it either: starting from the usual setup, is it possible to arrange the knights like this instead — diagonally, rather than side by side?

Try it on the board above before reading any further into why it might, or might not, be possible.
