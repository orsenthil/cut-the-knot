The Mind Reader

Puzzle by Alexander Bogomolny (cut-the-knot.org)

Game: see if I can guess the shape you're thinking of.
How to play: pick a number from 10 to 99. Take away both of its digits (for 47, do 47 − 4 − 7 = 36). Find your answer in the table, think hard about its shape, then press Read my mind!
Look for the math

There's no mind reading: your answer is decided before you even pick a number. Every 2-digit number lands on the same small set of answers, and the table gives all of them the same shape.

Taking away the digits leaves nine times the tens

By place value, a number with digits a and b is 10a + b. Take away its digits:

(10a + b) − (a + b) = 9a

The ones digit b cancels out completely, so only the tens digit matters. The answer is always nine times it: a multiple of 9. For 47 that's 9 × 4 = 36, and for 40, 41, … 49 it's 36 every time.

Only nine possible answers

The tens digit runs from 1 to 9, so the only answers are 9, 18, 27, 36, 45, 54, 63, 72, 81. Look for them in the table (press Reveal the secret): they all share one shape. The other cells get random shapes, and a few of those match by chance, which helps hide the pattern. A new table gets new shapes each round, so the trick doesn't give itself away.

Spotting a multiple of 9

You can check the answers with the divisibility rule for 9: a number is a multiple of 9 exactly when its digits add up to a multiple of 9 (3 + 6 = 9, 8 + 1 = 9). That works because 10 is one more than 9, so every ten is “nine plus one”. It's the same idea as casting out nines, and the one behind The Vanishing Digit.

Try it: what happens with a 3-digit number? Work out (100a + 10b + c) − (a + b + c). Is the answer still always a multiple of 9?
