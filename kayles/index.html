Kayles

Puzzle by Alexander Bogomolny (cut-the-knot.org)

Game: skittles stand in a ring and each turn you knock down one skittle, or two that stand side by side. Knock down the last one and you win.
How to play: click a skittle to knock it down, or the small dot between two standing skittles to knock down both.
Look for the math

Kayles was introduced by Henry Dudeney and Sam Loyd. Both players have the same moves and the last to move wins, so it is an impartial game, and it has the same hidden arithmetic as Nim.

The full circle: copy your opponent

With every skittle standing in a ring, the second player always wins. The first move, of one skittle or two, turns the ring into a single row. The second player knocks down the middle one or two of that row, leaving two equal rows, and from then on copies every move on the other row. Whoever copies always has a move, so the copier makes the last one. This mirror strategy is the one Martin Gardner describes. Tick "Full circle", press "Computer starts", and beat the computer by copying it.

Rows have numbers

When skittles are already missing, the ring breaks into separate rows, and they don't affect each other. Each row of n skittles has a Grundy number, the smallest value none of its moves can reach:

G(n) = mex { G(a) XOR G(n−1−a), G(a) XOR G(n−2−a) }

The player to move is losing exactly when the rows' numbers, combined by XOR, give 0. The computer uses this: if the total isn't 0 it finds a move that makes it 0, and otherwise plays at random and hopes you slip. A brute-force search of every position on rings of up to 14 skittles agrees with the XOR rule.

A surprise in the numbers

The applet carries a table of 84 Grundy numbers, and the mex rule reproduces all of them. Look further and a pattern appears: from rows of 71 skittles on, the numbers repeat every 12, forever, which Richard Guy and Cedric Smith proved. So Kayles on any number of skittles is completely solved.

Without "Full circle", the board starts with a gap or two. Work out the rows' lengths before your first move: if their XOR is already 0, press "Computer starts".
