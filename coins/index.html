Coins in a Row

Puzzle by Alexander Bogomolny (cut-the-knot.org)

Use
Goal: end up with more money than the computer.
How to play: take turns picking a coin from one end of the row (the glowing coins). Whoever collects more wins. Press "Make Move" if you want the computer to go first.
Look for the math

This is a browser version of Alexander Bogomolny's applet "A Game With Coins", after a puzzle in Peter Winkler's Mathematical Puzzles: A Connoisseur's Collection (A K Peters, 2004). The rules:

"The coins of various denominations are arranged in a chain with two end points. A move consists in removing an end coin, whereby the coin's immediate neighbor becomes an end coin available for removal on successive moves. When all coins have been removed, the player with the larger amount wins."

An even number of coins: the first player can't lose

"Number the coins 1, 2, ..., 2n. Sum the odd numbered denominations and separately the even numbered ones. If one sum is greater, the first player wins by sticking with those numbered coins. This is always possible."

The trick is a matter of parity. In a row of 2n coins the two ends always have different numbers: one odd, one even. Say the even-numbered coins are worth more, so you take coin 2n. Now both ends of the remaining row (coins 1 and 2n−1) are odd, so your opponent is forced to take an odd coin. Whichever one he takes, an even coin is showing at one end again — take it. This repeats all game long, so you collect every even coin and he collects every odd one. Since the bigger of the two sums can always be chosen, you get at least half the total. It's an invariant: each time it's your turn, one end has the parity you want.

That's exactly how the computer plays here. It adds up the two groups, picks the larger, and keeps taking end coins of that parity. If the parity rule doesn't decide (both ends or neither end fit), it just takes the bigger end coin, a greedy choice. Try it: with an even number of coins, press "Make Move" so the computer goes first — you can't beat it. Now play first yourself and use the same trick, and it can't beat you.

An odd number of coins: it depends

With coins "1, 5, 1" the first player loses. With "5, 1, 5" the first player wins easily.

With an odd row, the trick moves to the other side of the table. As soon as the first player takes a coin, an even number is left — and now the second player is the one who can claim the bigger parity group. The first player can only hope that the coin he just took is worth more than the advantage he handed over. In 1 – 5 – 1 it isn't (he takes a 1 and the 5 is lost); in 5 – 1 – 5 it is. For odd rows the best play depends on the actual numbers, and can be worked out by dynamic programming.

Arranged in a loop

"In a loop, the first player has more freedom to choose a coin: any coin will do whose number is of required parity."

Tick "Arrange in a loop" and there are no ends yet, so the first player may open the loop anywhere. Choosing a coin from the better parity group puts him in the same winning position as before. When the computer opens, it looks for a starting coin worth more than the edge its opponent would then get, and falls back to the biggest coin if none exists.
