Mini Lights Out

Puzzle by Alexander Bogomolny (cut-the-knot.org)

Game: turn every light off on a 4×4 board that wraps like a doughnut (the top row touches the bottom, and the sides wrap too).
How to play: click a light to flip it and its four neighbours. Hint rings the cells you need to press; Undo takes back a click.
Look for the math

Each press flips five cells on the torus. Over GF(2) (arithmetic mod 2), pressing twice cancels, and the order of presses does not matter. So a plan is just a 16-bit vector: press or don't, once each.

The move matrix squares to the identity

Line the sixteen cells up and let A be the 16×16 matrix whose j-th column is the pattern made by pressing cell j alone. A starting board v is cleared by a strategy s exactly when A s = v (mod 2). On this 4×4 torus one can check that A2 = I, so A is its own inverse and the unique solution is s = A v.

Parity of the neighbourhood

Multiplying by A just sums each cell's five-neighbourhood mod 2. So the Hint checkbox marks exactly the cells whose current parity (count of lit neighbours, including itself) is odd — and pressing those, in any order, turns the board off. Missigman and Weida proved the same rule by watching how presses change those parities: only pressing a cell flips its own parity.

Why the intersections matter

On this board, any two distinct plus-shaped neighbourhoods share either no cells or exactly two. Pressing another cell therefore changes a given cell's lit-count by an even number (0 or ±2), so it cannot flip that cell's parity — which is why the parity rule works so cleanly here.

Tick Hint, note the ringed cells, turn Hint off, and press only those. Every random board clears in at most sixteen clicks.
