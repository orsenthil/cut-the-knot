Euclid's Game

Puzzle by Alexander Bogomolny (cut-the-knot.org)

Game: two numbers on the grid are already marked. Take turns with the computer marking a new number that is the gap between two marked ones. Whoever can't move loses.
How to play: click a number to mark it. You go first, unless you let the computer start.

Look for the math

Taking differences over and over is the oldest algorithm there is: Euclid's algorithm for the greatest common divisor. This game is that algorithm with two players.

Where the game always ends

Call the two starting numbers a and b, and their greatest common divisor g. A difference of two multiples of g is again a multiple of g, and no bigger than the larger number, so only multiples of g up to the maximum can ever be marked. On the other hand, differences keep producing smaller numbers until they reach g itself, and from there every multiple of g up to the maximum. So every game ends in the same place:

g, 2g, 3g, …, max all marked, which is max / g numbers.

The winner is decided before the first move

Two numbers start marked, so every game lasts exactly max / g − 2 moves, whatever anyone plays. If that number is odd the first player makes the last move and wins; if it is even the second player wins. Checking all 10,296 starting pairs on the biggest grid, with random play, never finds a game that ends anywhere else or takes any other number of moves.

So the only real decision is who goes first, and the computer doesn't think at all: it marks the first new difference it finds. With 12 and 30, say, g = 6 and the game ends with 6, 12, 18, 24, 30: three moves, so start and you win.

Before your first move, work out max / g − 2. If it is even, press "Let the computer start".
