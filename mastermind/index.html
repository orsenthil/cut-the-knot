Mastermind

Puzzle by Alexander Bogomolny (cut-the-knot.org)

Game: break a secret code of coloured pegs. After each guess you learn how many pegs are exact (right colour, right place) and how many are near (right colour, wrong place).
How to play: click each peg to cycle its colour, then press Submit. Dark dots are exact matches; light dots are near matches.
Look for the math

With four pegs and six colours there are 64 = 1296 possible codes. Each guess carves that set down: only codes that would have given the same answer remain possible.

Exact and near, without double-counting

If a colour appears more often in the guess than in the secret, you must not credit every copy. Donald Knuth's clean rule: let ni be how often colour i appears in the code and mi how often in the guess. Colour i is matched min(ni, mi) times in total. Count the exact (same place) matches first; the near matches are whatever is left:

near = Σi min(ni, mi) − exact

So if the secret is 0145 and you guess 1123, there is one exact match (the second peg) and no near match — the extra 1 in the guess has already been used.

An impossible reply

You will never see three exact dots and one near dot together. With three pegs already pinned in place, the fourth peg is either exact too or a colour that is not in the secret at all — there is no peg left that could be “near.” Checking every code/guess pair for the smaller boards confirms it, and the same counting argument holds for any size.

How few guesses do you need?

Knuth showed that a careful strategy always finishes the classic game in at most five guesses (you know the code after at most four replies). A later strategy lowers the average number of guesses to about 4.34, at the cost of a worse worst case. The page also studies static Mastermind: choosing all guesses up front, with no feedback until the end — a cousin of the twelve-coin weighing puzzle.

Try a first guess like 0011 or 0012 (two colours, or three). Both are classic openings; afterwards, only keep codes that would have scored the same reply.
