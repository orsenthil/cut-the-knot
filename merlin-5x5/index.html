Lights Out

Puzzle by Alexander Bogomolny (cut-the-knot.org)

Game: turn every light off on a 5×5 board. Not every pattern can be cleared.
How to play: click a light to flip it and its neighbours. Hint rings a solution when one exists; Setup lets you flip single lights to build a board.
Look for the math

As in Mini Lights Out, presses commute and cancel in pairs, so the puzzle is a linear system A s = v over GF(2). Here the board is flat, not a torus, and A is singular: some boards have no solution, and solvable ones have four.

Chase the lights down

Whatever the starting pattern, you can always clear the top four rows: walking downward, press every cell that sits under a lit neighbour above. That gathering leaves at most the bottom row lit. The leftover five bits are a complete syndrome for the puzzle.

First-row presses, then gather again

Pressing a few buttons in the top row and gathering once more turns that syndrome into a known pattern. Experiment shows you never need the last two top buttons, and the map from bottom bits 1–3 to top presses 1–3 is tiny. The three-step recipe — gather, press those top cells, gather — either clears the board or proves it cannot be cleared.

When is it solvable?

After the first gathering, look at the bottom row. The board can be solved if and only if that row has even parity on columns {1, 3, 5} and on {2, 3, 4} (counting from 1). Those are exactly the two conditions forced by the two-dimensional nullspace of A (so rank(A) = 23, and a quarter of all boards work). When a solution exists there are four of them, because you may add any of the four “neutral” first-row sets and gather without changing the outcome.

Tick Hint on a random board: if rings appear, press them; if you see “No solution”, try Setup and change a light in the bottom row’s {1,3,5} set, then Hint again.
