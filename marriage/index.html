Marriage Problem

Puzzle by Alexander Bogomolny (cut-the-knot.org)

What it shows: a constructive proof of Hall's marriage theorem — when n girls each list some of n boys, can every girl marry a different boy from her list?
How to play: click cells to toggle likes (1) and blanks (0). Press Step, Slow, or Fast to run the algorithm, or click row/column labels to swap them yourself.
Algorithm log
    Look for the math

    Each column is one girl's list of boys (the rows marked 1). A matching that marries every girl to a different boy from her list is a system of distinct representatives — after swapping rows and columns, a diagonal of 1s.

    Hall's condition

    Hall's marriage theorem says such a matching exists if and only if every group of k girls, taken together, lists at least k different boys. The algorithm on this page finds a matching whenever that condition holds, and stops with a clear failure when it does not.

    Growing a diagonal of 1s

    W. McWorter's procedure keeps a block of 1s already fixed on the diagonal and looks in the remaining lower-right block for another 1 to bring onto the next diagonal place (the Greedy phase). When that block is all zeros it tries a careful column swap, or regroups columns, or shrinks the problem — always either extending the diagonal or proving Hall's condition fails.

    A matrix that works only for even n

    Put a 1 in every square next to the diagonal and 0s elsewhere (aij = 1 exactly when |i − j| = 1). For even n the algorithm finishes; for odd n it fails. That is exactly when Hall's condition holds: an odd-sized board leaves one girl short of a partner among the tight pairs along the strip.

    Reset until you get a messy board, press Fast, and watch whether you land on “Finished, all are happy” or “Failed, the life's hard.” Then try the even/odd strip by hand on size 4 and size 5.
