What I've done so far
    Goal: cook the egg for exactly 15 minutes. You have no clock — only the two sand timers!
    How to play: turn a timer over and its sand starts falling. Time stops by itself when a timer runs out — then take as long as you like to decide. Put the egg in or take it out at the right moment, or press "Continue" to let the rest of the sand fall. To start both timers together, turn them one right after the other.
    Look for the math

    This is a game version of Alexander Bogomolny's hourglass problem:

    "For a perfect breakfast, a fellow decides to boil an egg exactly 15 minutes. He has two hourglasses — one for 7 minutes, another for 11. How should he go about preparing his breakfast? How many times will he have to turn hourglasses? What would be the minimal required number of glass turns?"

    Bogomolny gives four different solutions, and the point he makes is that formal knowledge alone doesn't lead you to the best one. Here's what's going on underneath.

    What turning a glass really does

    However the sand is spread, the amount on top plus the amount at the bottom is always the glass's full time — an invariant. So an hourglass is really two timers in one: the top counts down while the bottom counts up. Turning it swaps them. A 7-minute glass with 3 minutes on top and 4 at the bottom, turned over, now has 4 minutes to run — the time that had already passed becomes the time that remains. That is the trick behind John Flynn's solution (#4): flip the 7-minute glass when the 11 runs out, and it finishes exactly 15 minutes after you started.

    The shortest recipe: 2b − a

    With glasses of a and b minutes (b > a), start both. When the small one runs out, the big one has b − a minutes left. Start the egg right then. When the big glass runs out, turn it and wait another b minutes:

    (b − a) + b = 2b − a

    For 7 and 11 that's 4 + 11 = 15. The same recipe works for 5 and 9 (2·9 − 5 = 13) and 8 and 12 (2·12 − 8 = 16) — though, as Bogomolny notes with a smile, the last one has a trivial answer: use the 8-minute glass twice. A general solution isn't always the best one.

    About counting flips. Bogomolny doesn't count the flips that first start the glasses. His version of this recipe also turns the small glass at the 7-minute mark, which a reader pointed out is redundant — leaving one real turn (the big glass at 11 minutes), which he calls an absolute minimum. This game counts every flip, including the two that start the glasses, so the same recipe scores 3 here. Press "Show a shortest solution" to see it.

    Making exactly one minute

    His first solution is more formal. It uses the identity 11·2 − 7·3 = 1: run the big glass twice (22 minutes) and the small glass three times (21 minutes) and they finish exactly one minute apart. Once you can make a single minute, 15 = 1 + 14, and 14 is just two runs of the 7-minute glass. It works for two reasons: 7 and 11 share no common factor, so Bézout's identity guarantees whole numbers X and Y with 11X − 7Y = 1. But by his count it takes 5 turns — more than the cleverer answers above.

    When it can't be done

    Every moment a glass runs out is a whole-number combination of the two sizes, so it's always a multiple of their greatest common divisor. The egg goes in at one such moment and comes out at another, so the time it cooks is a multiple of that divisor too. Glasses of 4 and 6 minutes only ever tick in even minutes — they can never boil an egg for 5. Try it: choose "Make your own…", set the glasses to 4 and 6 and the egg to 5, then press "Show a shortest solution".

    How the game finds the shortest way

    There are only so many situations the puzzle can be in: how much sand is on top of each glass, whether the egg is in the pot, and how long it has cooked. The game explores all of them with Dijkstra's shortest-path algorithm, ranking a way of doing it first by the fewest flips, then by the least waiting. For 7 and 11 that gives the 3-flip recipe above; if instead you value getting the egg in at once, John Flynn's version costs one more flip but finishes 7 minutes sooner.
