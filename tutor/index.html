How a session goes

  1. Play first. You open a game and start. The teacher does not explain the rules of the trick, only the rules of the game.
  2. Guess and test. You say what you think will work. The teacher asks why, and you try it.
  3. Notice a pattern. After a few plays, the teacher asks what stayed the same every time.
  4. Name the idea. Only now do you meet the maths word for it (parity, an invariant, a recurrence), and open Look for the math on the page to see it written down.
  5. Try a new case. You use the idea on a bigger or different puzzle, to see that it really works.

What the teacher might do

With the hourglass puzzle (measuring time):

“You have a 7-minute and an 11-minute hourglass and need 15 minutes. Don’t tell me the answer yet. Just flip one and tell me what you notice when the sand runs out.”

“What is the smallest amount of time you can measure now? Could you ever measure 1 minute?”

The idea you leave with: sand left over in one glass is a subtraction, and subtractions reach every number the two sizes allow.

With the Tower of Hanoi:

“Try it with two discs. Now three. How many moves each? Write them down.”

“To move four discs, what must you do with the top three first?”

The idea you leave with: a big problem hides a smaller copy of itself, which is what makes 1, 3, 7, 15… (one less than a power of two).

With the coin game:

“Play against the computer a few times. Does it ever lose? What do you think it is looking at?”

“Colour the coins in odd and even places. Now what does the first player know?”

The idea you leave with: counting in even and odd positions gives the first player a plan that never loses.

What it is good for

  • Getting unstuck on a puzzle without being handed the answer.
  • Learning to explain why something works, not only that it does.
  • Building habits for problem solving and competitions: try small cases, look for what does not change, check your answer.
  • Following your own record: the teacher can look at your profile (if you show it) to choose the next game.
