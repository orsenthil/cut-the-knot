Toads and Frogs

Puzzle by Alexander Bogomolny (cut-the-knot.org)

Game: green frogs on the left, brown toads on the right, one empty square between them. Win by getting all the toads to the left end and all the frogs to the right end.
How to play: click an animal to move it. Frogs only go right and toads only go left, into the empty square or in one hop over a single animal of the other kind.
Look for the math

This old puzzle is also sold as Hares and Tortoises, Sheep and Goats, or Traffic Jam. Bogomolny's applet writes down every move as you play: S for a slide, J for a jump. Those letters turn out to say a lot about the puzzle.

How many moves

With N frogs and M toads, every frog has to travel M+1 squares right and every toad N+1 squares left. A slide covers one square and a jump covers two. A jump is the only way a frog and a toad can get past each other, and every frog must pass every toad once, so there are exactly MN jumps:

moves = N(M+1) + M(N+1) − MN = MN + M + N

Every solution takes exactly that many moves: there is no shorter way and, if you finish at all, no longer one.

The moves read the same backwards

Play a finished solution backwards and it solves the puzzle again, with the animals' roles swapped. That is why the string of Ss and Js is always a palindrome. Three of each gives SJSJJSJJJSJJSJS. With at least two animals on each side there are exactly two solutions, one starting each way, and they write the same string even when N and M differ.

Every move is forced

Two jumps on offer at once is fatal: whichever you take, you are stuck soon after. Facing a jump and a slide, always jump. Facing two slides, pick the one that does not set up two jumps. Checking every position on every board up to five a side confirms what the page says: after the first move, only one move ever keeps the puzzle solvable.

Except when one side has a single animal. Then there are many solutions: 3, 5, 8 and 13 of them for 2, 3, 4 and 5 animals on the other side. Those are the Fibonacci numbers.

Following the hole

Tick "Hole moves" to record the empty square instead: r or R when it moves one or two squares right, l or L for left. The two solutions now give different strings. Bogomolny's page says they are palindromes only when M = N, but checking every board up to five a side shows more: with two or more animals on each side, the hole's string is a palindrome exactly when N + M is even. Three frogs and five toads give lRrLLlRRRrLLLrRRRlLLrRl.

Try two frogs and four toads with "Hole moves" ticked, then two frogs and three toads. Can you see why the even case reads the same both ways?
